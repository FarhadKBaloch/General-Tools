# Driver vehicle inspection reports — a QR sticker on every truck

The pre-trip inspection sheet, turned into something a driver fills in on a
phone in the yard. It costs nothing, needs no server, and takes about half an
hour to set up.

A driver scans the sticker on the truck they are taking out. The app opens
already knowing it is Brutus, shows them anything the last driver reported that
nobody has fixed yet, and gives them the same checklist that is on the paper
form. They tap anything that is wrong, write what they saw, sign with a finger,
and submit. The shop gets an email within seconds, and the report lands as a row
in a spreadsheet.

Adding the spring trucks is typing their names on a tab and printing more
stickers. No code change, no redeploy.

---

## The one thing not to redesign

On the paper form, a tick means **something is wrong**. An untouched sheet is a
truck that passed.

That is backwards from almost every checklist app, and it is tempting to "fix"
it. Do not. Your drivers have been filling this sheet for years, and the muscle
memory is what it is. The app keeps the paper meaning — tick only the defects —
and says so on screen, in the section header count, and in the colour of the
row. A ticked item turns red, not green.

The trade is that a driver who taps nothing has not obviously done anything. So
the app will not accept a blank report: either items are ticked, or the driver
confirms *"Truck is in satisfactory condition — no defects observed"*, which is
the same checkbox that is on the paper. One or the other, never neither, never
both.

## What you're building

| Piece | What it is |
|---|---|
| A Google Sheet | The log. One row per inspection, kept forever. |
| `dvir-notify.gs` | The script, pasted into that sheet's Apps Script project. |
| `dvir-webapp.html` | The driver app, pasted in as an HTML file named `webapp`. |
| A web app deployment | The URL the stickers point at. Google hosts it, free. |
| QR stickers | One per truck, generated by `equipment-qr-labels.html`. |
| `dvir.test.js` | Checks for the script's rules. Not deployed — see [Changing the checklist](#changing-the-checklist). |

Keep this in **its own Apps Script project**, separate from the nursery's
equipment maintenance log. Two `doGet()` functions cannot live in one project,
and the two logs have different readers anyway.

## Step 1 — Make the sheet and paste the code (10 minutes)

1. Create a new Google Sheet. Name it something like *Truck inspections*. It
   does **not** need a Google Form — the web app is the form.
2. **Extensions → Apps Script**. Delete the `function myFunction() {}` stub.
3. Paste all of `dvir-notify.gs` in. Rename the file `dvir-notify` if you like.
4. **File → New → HTML file**, name it exactly `webapp` (no `.html`), delete
   what Google puts there, and paste all of `dvir-webapp.html` in.
5. Edit the `CONFIG` block at the top — see below.
6. Run `setUp` from the toolbar and accept the permission prompt. Google will
   warn you the app is unverified; it is your own script, so continue.

### Settings to change

At minimum:

```js
shopLead: 'whoever.fixes.trucks@millcreekplants.com',
owner:    'owner@millcreekplants.com',
trucks:   ['Brutus', 'Buckeye', 'Benz'],
```

Everything else has a working default. The ones worth a second look:

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| `alertItems` | Defects on these flag the email high-importance and put a ⚠ in the subject. Defaults to brakes, steering and tires. |
| `certifiers` | Who may mark a defect repaired. Empty means anyone who can open the app — right for a small shop, see [Who can close a defect](#who-can-close-a-defect). |
| `safetyContact` | An extra address, emailed only for `alertItems`. Blank sends those to the same people as everything else. |
| `workEmailDomain` | Fills the driver's name in automatically when they're signed in with a work account. Only works on a domain-restricted deployment. |
| `staleAfterHours` | A truck with no inspection in this long is flagged **overdue** on the Fleet screen. Default 36. |
| `signatureFolderId` | Where signature PNGs go. Leave blank and it makes a folder called *DVIR signatures*. |

`setUp` creates two tabs:

- **Inspections** — the log. Twenty columns, colour-coded by repair status.
- **Trucks** — the fleet list, seeded from `CONFIG.trucks`. From now on **this
  tab is the source of truth**, not the code.

## Step 2 — Deploy the web app (5 minutes)

**Deploy → New deployment → Web app.**

| Field | Set it to | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Execute as | **Me** | So the script can write to the sheet and Drive whoever is using it. |
| Who has access | **Anyone** *or* **Anyone within your organisation** | See below. |

**Anyone** means the sticker works for any driver with a phone, signed in or
not. That is usually what you want — a driver at 5am should not be locked out
of a safety inspection by a Google login. The cost is that Google will not tell
the script who the viewer is, so drivers type their name and `certifiers` can't
be enforced.

**Anyone within your organisation** gives you the driver's identity on every
report and lets `certifiers` work, but every driver needs a work Google account
on their phone.

Copy the URL it gives you. It ends in `/exec`.

> A `/dev` URL only works for you, while signed in. Stickers printed with a
> `/dev` URL will fail for everyone else. Always use `/exec`.

## Step 3 — Print the stickers (10 minutes)

Open `equipment-qr-labels.html` in any browser. It runs entirely in the page —
nothing is uploaded anywhere.

1. **Link source**: *An Apps Script web app, one name per line*.
2. **Web app link**: paste the `/exec` URL from step 2.
3. **Link parameter**: change `equipment` to **`truck`**.
4. **Names**: one truck per line, spelled exactly as on the Trucks tab.

```
Brutus
Buckeye
Benz
```

Check the grey line under the URL reads `…/exec?truck=Brutus`. Then
**Generate**, then **Print**.

### Making them survive a truck

A sticker on a truck has a harder life than one on a greenhouse bench. What
works:

- Print on white weatherproof label stock, or laminate and use outdoor
  double-sided tape.
- Put it **inside the cab**, on the driver's door pillar or the dash. A sticker
  on the outside gets road salt, pressure washers and sun, and a QR code that
  has faded 20% stops scanning.
- One per truck. If a truck gets renamed, reprint — the name in the QR code has
  to match the Trucks tab exactly.
- Keep the PDF. You will reprint.

## Step 4 — Check it end to end (5 minutes)

1. From the spreadsheet, **Inspections → Health check**. It reports on the log,
   the fleet list, the checklist, email, the signature folder and the triggers,
   and tells you what to fix rather than stopping at the first problem.
2. **Inspections → Send a test email** to prove the addresses work.
3. Scan a sticker with an actual phone. File a satisfactory report, then file
   one with a defect, and check the email arrives.
4. Scan the same truck again — the defect you just filed should be sitting at
   the top of the screen with a tick box under it.

## How it runs day to day

### The driver

Scan → the truck's name is already at the top of the screen.

**Pre-trip** is selected by default. If the last driver left a defect open, it
is the first thing on the page, in red, with *"I have reviewed the defects
listed above"* underneath. The app will not submit until that is ticked. This is
the part of the federal rule that paper handles worst — the previous sheet is in
a binder in the office, and nobody goes and gets it.

Then: name (remembered after the first time), mileage, and the checklist. Four
sections, twenty-five items, in the same order as the paper. Left/right items
get an **L** and an **R** button, same as the L | R column on the sheet.

Tap anything wrong. The section header counts what you've ticked, the running
tally sits at the bottom of the screen, and the submit button turns red and
reads **Submit 3 defects** so nobody submits a defect report thinking they filed
a clean one.

Remarks are required as soon as anything is ticked — *"check any defective item
and provide details"* is the whole point, and a defect with no description
leaves the shop guessing.

Sign with a finger. Submit.

**Post-trip** is the same screen with the type switched and the mileage field
relabelled. On paper, post-trip is just a remarks box and a signature; here it
keeps the checklist, because end of day is when a driver has actually found out
whether the wipers work, and a defect found at 5pm needs somewhere structured to
go.

### The shop

The **Fleet** tab lists every truck, when it was last inspected, who by, and
what is outstanding. A truck nobody has inspected in 36 hours is marked
**overdue**.

Under that, every open defect report in full, each with a box to close it out:

- **Repaired** — with a note saying what was done.
- **No repair needed** — with a note saying why. The app refuses a bare "no
  repair needed" with nothing next to it, because that is not a record of
  anything.

Either one clears the truck's flag, and the next driver stops seeing the
warning. Nothing else does — a defect does not age out, and a driver cannot
close their own report by filing a clean one the next morning.

### The spreadsheet

One row per inspection, newest at the bottom, append-only. Rows are coloured by
repair status: red needs work, green was repaired, grey needed none.

The `Items (JSON)` column on the far right is the machine-readable copy of what
was ticked. Ignore it; it is there so a future version of this can chart which
items fail most without re-parsing English.

Editing **Repair status** by hand in the sheet works, and stamps who and when.

## Adding the spring trucks

Open the **Trucks** tab. Type the new names, one per row. That is the whole
change — the app reads the tab on every page load.

Then print stickers for them (step 3) with the same `/exec` URL.

To take a truck off the list without losing its history, put any word in the
**Retired?** column. It stops being offered to drivers; its past reports stay in
the log and it still shows up on the Fleet screen under "also in the history".

Fifteen trucks is comfortably inside every limit that matters — see
[What this costs](#what-this-costs-and-where-the-limits-are).

## Changing the checklist

The checklist lives in `CONFIG.sections` in `dvir-notify.gs`. Sections and items
appear in the order they are written. Add `sides: true` to an item to give it
L and R buttons.

```js
{ label: 'Liftgate operational' },
{ label: 'Mirrors', sides: true },
```

Save the script and reload the app — no redeploy needed for a content change,
though a new deployment is needed if you change anything else in the file.

Renaming an item does not rewrite old reports. History keeps the wording that
was on screen when the driver signed it, which is rather the point of a signed
record.

If you add an item that belongs in `alertItems`, add it there too, spelled
identically. `healthCheck` will tell you if it doesn't match anything.

### Checking you haven't broken anything

`dvir.test.js` runs the script's rules against a stubbed-out Apps Script — what
a submission is allowed to contain, which defects still count as open, what
closing one out requires. It needs Node and nothing else:

```bash
node dvir.test.js
```

It does not talk to Google, so a pass says the rules are right, not that the
deployment works. `healthCheck` from the spreadsheet menu is what says that.

## Who can close a defect

`CONFIG.certifiers` is empty by default, which means anyone who can open the app
can mark a defect repaired. For a three-truck operation where everyone knows
everyone, that is the right setting: the record of *who* signed the
certification is the accountability, not a permission check.

To lock it down, list the addresses:

```js
certifiers: ['shop.lead@millcreekplants.com', 'owner@millcreekplants.com'],
workEmailDomain: 'millcreekplants.com',
```

**This only works if the deployment's access is "Anyone within your
organisation".** Deployed as "Anyone", every viewer is anonymous, Google tells
the script nothing, and the list would lock out everybody including you.
`healthCheck` warns about exactly this combination.

## What this does and does not claim

This app keeps the record the paper form kept, and adds the two things paper is
worst at: putting the last driver's open defects in front of the next driver,
and making the repair certification a step somebody has to actually take.

It is **not** a compliance product and nobody here is your DOT lawyer. What it
does:

- Records who inspected which truck, when, what they found, and their signature.
- Keeps the defect open until someone certifies it repaired or certifies that no
  repair was needed, and records who did that and when.
- Shows the next driver the open defects and records that they reviewed them.
- Keeps everything in a spreadsheet you own, indefinitely. Reports and repair
  certifications generally have to be kept for three months; the app tells
  drivers that on screen and never deletes anything itself.

What it does not do:

- Decide whether a truck is safe to drive. `alertItems` puts a ⚠ on an email
  about brakes; it is a prompt for a person, not an out-of-service call.
- Handle trailers separately from the truck they're behind.
- Work offline. See [Limits](#what-this-costs-and-where-the-limits-are).
- Replace whatever your insurer or your auditor specifically asks for. Show them
  a printed report before you throw the paper pad away.

## What this costs and where the limits are

Nothing. It runs inside Google's free quota for a consumer or Workspace account.

| Limit | Free / Workspace | What 15 trucks actually uses |
|---|---|---|
| Emails per day | 100 / 1,500 | Only defect reports email. A bad week is maybe 10. |
| Script runtime | 6 min per run | A submission is well under a second. |
| Drive storage | 15 GB+ | A signature PNG is ~15 KB. 15 trucks × 2/day ≈ 160 MB/year. |
| Sheet cells | 10 million | 20 columns × 30 rows/day ≈ 220k cells/year. |

The two real limits:

**Cold start.** An Apps Script web app that has not been used in a while takes
2–4 seconds to wake up. The first driver of the morning waits; everyone after
them doesn't.

**No offline.** A phone with no signal cannot submit. The app does not clear the
form on a failed send — the checklist, the remarks and the signature are all
still there, and the driver taps submit again once they have a bar. But they do
have to be somewhere with signal before the record exists. If your yard is a
dead spot, that is worth knowing before you retire the paper pad.

## Troubleshooting

**"Could not load" when a sticker is scanned.** Usually a `/dev` URL on the
sticker, or a deployment that was never made "Anyone". Open the URL yourself in
a private window — if you can't see it, neither can a driver.

**The truck name isn't recognised and the app shows a picker.** The name in the
QR code doesn't match the Trucks tab exactly — check for a trailing space or a
different capitalisation. Reprint the sticker, or fix the tab.

**Nobody got the email.** Run `Inspections → Health check`. Most often the
`example.com` placeholders are still in CONFIG, or the daily email quota is
spent.

**A defect won't clear.** It clears from the Fleet screen, not from the sheet —
marking it in the sheet works too, but only in the **Repair status** column, and
only with one of the four exact values in the dropdown.

**Two reports got the same ID.** Two drivers submitted in the same second and
the lock was busy. Harmless — the rows are both there and the timestamps differ.
Renumber by hand if it bothers you.

**A signature is missing from a row.** Check the *DVIR signatures* folder
exists and the account that owns the deployment can write to it. The report is
saved before the email is sent and after the signature, so a saved row with no
signature link means the Drive write failed — `healthCheck` reports on the
folder.

## If you outgrow this

The honest upgrade path, in order of when you'd want it:

1. **Photos on a defect.** The single most useful thing missing. A driver
   photographs the cracked lens instead of describing it. Perhaps 40 lines,
   copying the pattern already in `maintenance-notify.gs`.
2. **Trailer inspections** as their own record, linked to the truck.
3. **A real DVIR/ELD product** once you're running enough trucks that somebody's
   full-time job is fleet compliance. At that point the spreadsheet is the thing
   you export *from*, and the three months of history in it is the reason the
   migration is easy.
